# Style 1 — Genesis Case Study

This is the saved visual and storytelling direction for the current Genesis case study. When the user says **“use Style 1”**, preserve this direction unless they explicitly ask to change a specific part.

## Visual direction

- White / warm off-white background only.
- Keep the original cinematic Genesis hero section with the large Genesis dashboard treatment.
- Editorial, premium, minimal product-design portfolio feel.
- Strong whitespace and deliberate pacing between sections.
- Soft neutral surfaces; no dark-theme case-study treatment.
- Restrained blue / purple / cyan accents associated with Genesis.
- Large visual moments should carry the page instead of turning every section into a card grid.
- Smooth, subtle motion; animation should explain or reinforce the story, not become decoration.

## Typography hierarchy

1. Large statement / section headline — strongest visual weight.
2. Section title — clear and prominent.
3. Supporting explanation — readable body copy.
4. Metadata / labels — small and restrained.

Do not make labels, body copy, and section headings visually compete with one another.

## Storytelling direction

The case study should read as a coherent product-design story:

**What is Genesis? → What problem was I solving? → What was the design question? → How did I structure the solution? → Why did I make these decisions? → How does the product work? → How did I create trust and control around AI? → What did the work achieve / teach me?**

Every major section should answer why the viewer is seeing it and how it connects to the previous section.

## Content principles

- Explain the product before showing detailed UI.
- Introduce the problem before presenting the solution.
- Use real project facts only; do not invent research findings, usability-test results, personas, or business metrics.
- When evidence is unavailable, frame the section around design reasoning rather than fabricated outcomes.
- Pair important claims with a visual, screen, diagram, video, or interaction when possible.
- Prefer a few strong screens with annotations and narrative over many disconnected screenshots.

## Case-study rhythm

**Context → Problem → Design question → Solution overview → Detailed design decisions → Product experience → Trust / states → Outcome & learning.**

This is the canonical **Style 1** direction for future Genesis case-study changes.
