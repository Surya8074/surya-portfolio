Introduction

LaundroMart brings garment records, transaction flows, operator workspaces, facility management, camera setup and reporting into one connected operations experience. The goal was to make a broad set of day-to-day tasks feel like one coherent product, with a clear overview and consistent patterns as users move into the details.

Where I came in

My work focused on shaping a connected experience across LaundroMart’s operational workflows. The interface needed to make garment information, transactions, operator tasks and facility tools easier to navigate while keeping the product visually consistent.

I approached the screens as parts of one system: clear page hierarchy for quick scanning, repeatable patterns for records and forms, and consistent navigation between overview-level information and task-level detail.

My contributions

As the sole product designer, I shaped LaundroMart from research and workflow mapping through interface design and a consistent design system.

  • Product research:Identified user needs, operational pain points and workflow challenges.
  • User journeys:Mapped key tasks and structured the information architecture across the product.
  • Core screens:Designed operational views, records, forms and task-level details for clearer day-to-day use.
  • UX patterns and interactions:Created consistent navigation, feedback and interaction patterns across workflows.
  • Design system:Defined reusable components, typography, spacing and visual rules for a cohesive interface.
  • Prototyping and iteration:Built and refined concepts to communicate design decisions and align with stakeholders.

Understanding the laundry business ecosystem.

When I started working on LaundroMart, I first needed to understand how a laundry business runs day to day. I explored the operational workflow—from garment intake and order tracking to staff tasks, facilities and delivery—and how each part affects the next.

That helped me see the product beyond individual screens. The real challenge was understanding how people, garments and operational information move through one connected business.

Learning from the workflow

I focused on the key tasks and information people need to manage laundry operations. I mapped the journey across garment records, transactions, operators and facilities to understand where information needs to be captured, checked and acted on.

As I worked through these flows, I kept returning to three questions:

What does the team need to see first?

Which garment or order details help them make the next decision?

Where can missing or unclear information interrupt the workflow?

The design direction became clearer: the interface needed to make operational information easier to scan, keep garment and order records accessible, and help users move between tasks without losing context.

What the customer told me

To understand the real needs behind the product, I wanted to learn from the people involved in everyday laundry operations. I focused on their routines, the information they depend on, and the moments where managing garments and orders becomes difficult.

These conversations and workflow discussions helped me look beyond assumptions and consider how the system could better support the people using it.

I separated owner and operator access because their responsibilities are different. Owners can manage facilities, configure cameras, review records and download reports, while operators only see the orders and evidence needed for their daily work.

Keeping the operator interface focused reduces unnecessary complexity and makes daily tasks easier to learn, helping staff get started with minimal training.

THE CORE WORKFLOW

From garment intake to operational visibility

LaundroMart connects daily laundry operations with business oversight. From garment records and order activity to facility monitoring and reporting, the experience brings operational information together so staff can manage their work and owners can understand what is happening across the business.

01 — OPERATOR WORKFLOW

Keep the order in context.

When an operator opens an order, the details need to bring the work into focus. The order summary connects the operator and wash dates with clothing counts, status, and the garment evidence associated with that record.

Rather than explaining each control in isolation, this view gives the operator a place to review the order and understand the information available before moving into a more specific task.

A CLOSER LOOK

Review, organise and correct the record.

The supporting views show how operators can add a transaction, inspect garment evidence, and change a category when a correction is needed. Together, these screens explain more of the workflow than the dashboard alone.

TRANSACTION CAPTURE

Keep the wash record specific.

The transaction form captures the wash category and camera selection alongside the transaction identifier. These fields help define the context for the record being added.

EVIDENCE REVIEW

Make corrections part of the workflow.

The evidence view groups clothing by category, while the change-category dialog provides a way to move selected evidence when it has been assigned incorrectly.

02 — OWNER EXPERIENCE

See the business at a glance.

The owner dashboard brings operational activity into one overview. Instead of checking individual orders to understand how the business is moving, the owner can start with the key volumes and trends, then look more closely at performance and garment mix.

The hierarchy moves from headline totals to visual summaries: received, completed and pending work first, followed by total-clothes activity over time, before/after-wash comparisons and clothing-type distribution.

A CLOSER LOOK

Move from headline metrics to operational patterns.

The dashboard combines three levels of information: summary metrics for a quick status check, a trend line for activity over time, and supporting charts for daily performance and clothing-type distribution. This gives the owner several ways to read the same operation without making every metric compete for attention.

AT-A-GLANCE STATUS

Put the most immediate numbers first.

Received, completed and pending volumes sit at the top of the page. Their placement helps the owner establish a quick status before reading the more detailed charts below.

PERFORMANCE CONTEXT

Give totals more than one point of reference.

The trend line, daily before/after-wash comparison and clothing-type chart show different views of operational activity. Together they help the owner move beyond a single total and inspect how the work is distributed.

↗ Design decision

I placed the summary metrics before the charts so the owner can scan the current workload first, then explore patterns in the supporting visualisations. The page progresses from quick status to deeper context rather than presenting every data point with equal emphasis.

02.2 — OPERATOR MANAGEMENT

Manage the people behind the workflow.

The operator directory gives the owner one place to find staff records. Search, working-status labels and operator details support scanning, while the add, edit and view actions provide direct paths to common management tasks.

Opening an operator profile continues the workflow from the staff list to that person's details and order history.

A CLOSER LOOK

Keep staff actions close to each record.

The directory groups each operator's identity, status and contact details together, with actions placed at the bottom of the record. This makes the available next step clear without opening every profile just to find a management action.

↗ Design decision

I kept operator discovery and management in the same directory. Search supports finding a record, status labels make availability easier to scan, and the view action leads into the individual profile when more context is needed.

02.3 — OPERATOR PROFILE & ORDER HISTORY

Connect each operator to the work they handle.

The operator profile brings personal details and transaction activity into one view. A total transaction count provides a quick summary, while the order table lets the owner inspect individual records by order ID, operator, wash dates, clothing count, status and downloadable record.

Keeping the order history alongside the profile gives the owner a more direct way to review work associated with a specific operator.

A CLOSER LOOK

Make individual transactions easier to inspect.

The order table presents the key details in consistent columns, with completion states visible in the status column and a download action available for each row. This supports record-level review without separating the operator context from the work history.

↗ Design decision

I kept the profile summary and order table within the same screen so the owner can move from an operator-level overview to individual records without losing context. Consistent columns and explicit status labels make the table easier to scan row by row.

03 — FACILITY & CAMERA MANAGEMENT

One system. Multiple facilities. Clearer oversight.

Owners managing more than one laundry location need a clear way to keep facility information and camera setup organised. This workflow brings the locations into one place, then lets the owner move from a facility record to its cameras and surveillance view.

A CLOSER LOOK

Start with the location, then manage its setup.

Each facility is represented as its own record, with contact information and a visible camera count. The owner can scan the list, add a facility or open a location to continue managing its camera setup.

FACILITY OVERVIEW

Make locations easy to scan.

Facility cards keep the location’s key details together, including contact information and camera count. The Add Facility action supports adding another location without mixing facility records together.

CAMERA CONFIGURATION

Keep camera setup in context.

Camera thumbnails and the Add Camera action are presented in the facility workflow, making it clear which location the owner is configuring and which cameras are associated with it.

SURVEILLANCE VIEW

Give the selected camera the focus.

The surveillance screen prioritises a large viewing area, with camera thumbnails available for switching between views. The interface keeps camera selection close to the feed being reviewed.

OWNER CONTROL

Move between setup and review.

The overall flow connects facility records, camera configuration and surveillance access, so the owner can move from managing the location to checking the camera view within the same operational area.

↗ Design decision

I kept facility information as the starting point and grouped cameras within that location’s context. The facility list supports quick scanning, camera thumbnails make the setup visible, and the larger surveillance area gives the selected feed clear priority.

04 — REPORTING & EVIDENCE

Make every order easier to verify.

The reporting workflow connects the order list with the garment summary and its supporting evidence. Owners and operators can review the order context, understand the garment counts, and open the detailed view when they need to check before-wash and after-wash records.

The downloaded report carries the key order information into a format that can be reviewed beyond the dashboard.

A CLOSER LOOK

Move from an order total to the evidence behind it.

The order list keeps the operational fields together. Opening an order reveals the total and category counts, garment groups, and separate before-wash and after-wash evidence areas.

ORDER SUMMARY

Keep the operational context together.

The order ID, operator, wash dates, garment count and completion status help the viewer identify the record before inspecting its contents.

GARMENT BREAKDOWN

Make category totals easy to scan.

The summary surfaces total garments and category count, while the detail view groups the garments by type. This gives the user a high-level count before moving into individual evidence.

BEFORE / AFTER EVIDENCE

Keep each stage clearly labelled.

Separate before-wash and after-wash areas make it easier to understand which evidence belongs to each stage, rather than combining all images into a single gallery.

DOWNLOAD REPORT

Carry the order record beyond the dashboard.

The download action provides access to a report containing order information, totals and garment rows for review outside the main interface.

↗ Design decision

I kept the reporting path layered: the order list supports scanning, the detail view connects garment categories to before-and-after evidence, and the downloadable report preserves key fields for review. Each level answers a different question without crowding one screen with every detail.

06 — DESIGN DECISIONS

Understanding the laundry business ecosystem.

The product supports two kinds of work: operators need to move through garment records with confidence, while owners need a broader view of people, facilities and activity. These decisions connect those needs without treating every screen as the same task.

01 / ACCESS
↗ Design decision

Separate daily execution from business oversight.

Operator and owner workflows serve different responsibilities. The operator view focuses on orders, transactions and garment evidence; owner-facing workflows extend to staff, facilities, cameras and reporting. Keeping these responsibilities distinct helps each role focus on the information needed for its work.

02 / INFORMATION HIERARCHY
↗ Design decision

Keep the order as the anchor.

Order details connect wash dates, clothing counts, status and captured garment evidence. Keeping these details in the same context gives operators a clearer point of reference before they create a transaction or review individual garments.

03 / ERROR RECOVERY
↗ Design decision

Make corrections part of the normal workflow.

Garment evidence is grouped by category, and the change-category interaction provides a way to correct a misclassification. Keeping that correction close to the evidence helps the operator resolve an issue in context rather than losing track of the item being reviewed.

04 / SYSTEM CONSISTENCY
↗ Design decision

Use predictable patterns across supporting tools.

Transactions, facility settings and reporting support the core garment workflow. Consistent navigation, labels and action placement help these secondary tasks feel connected to the same product rather than separate tools.

07 — OUTCOME & IMPACT

Clarity for customers. Traceability for owners.

LaundroMart connects garment-level evidence to owner-level oversight. The intent is to give staff a clearer reference when explaining an order and help owners trace the activity behind daily operations; the impact must be validated with service data.

01 / CUSTOMER TRUST

When there’s a concern, there’s a clearer story.

Order status, structured garment details and available item evidence give staff a shared point of reference when a customer asks what happened. The goal is not to promise that every issue disappears, but to make the information needed to investigate and explain it easier to find.
