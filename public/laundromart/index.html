Introduction

LaundroMart brings garment records, transaction flows, operator workspaces, facility management, camera setup and reporting into one connected operations experience. The goal was to make a broad set of day-to-day tasks feel like one coherent product, with a clear overview and consistent patterns as users move into the details.

Where I came in

My work focused on shaping a connected experience across LaundroMart’s operational workflows. The interface needed to make garment information, transactions, operator tasks and facility tools easier to navigate while keeping the product visually consistent.

I approached the screens as parts of one system: clear page hierarchy for quick scanning, repeatable patterns for records and forms, and consistent navigation between overview-level information and task-level detail.

01 — The challenge

The interface has to connect the whole operation.

Different work areas have different needs, but they should still feel familiar and connected—not like a collection of unrelated admin screens.

The design challenge

Design for overview and detail—without losing context.

The product moves between high-level activity, dense records, forms, individual operator views and technical camera settings. Each screen has a different job, but users should not have to relearn the interface at every step.

I focused on clear page hierarchy, predictable navigation, readable records and repeatable form patterns so the experience could support both quick scanning and task-level work.

03 — The experience

A connected system across multiple workflows.

This walkthrough follows the way the platform is organised—from the overview, into garment records, then into the people and infrastructure supporting the work.

01 / Inventory

Make garment records easy to scan and act on.

Inventory is where operational detail becomes actionable. A clear table structure, status cues and predictable actions help users move between records without losing context.

The layout prioritises scanning first, then provides a path into more detailed garment and order information.

02 / Transactions

Keep each operational step connected to its record.

Transaction flows need to capture the right information without overwhelming the user. The experience groups key fields into a clear sequence and makes the primary action easy to identify.

03 / People

Support both team-level and individual views.

The operator experience includes team management, an individual profile and an operator-facing dashboard. These views serve different levels of attention: finding a person, understanding their details and working within an individual view.

Consistent navigation and information patterns help users move between team-level oversight and individual context without feeling like they have entered a separate product.

04 / Facilities & cameras

Make the infrastructure behind the workflow manageable.

Facilities and camera settings are essential supporting workflows. Their screens need to make configuration, current setup and available actions understandable without distracting from the core task.

Grouping related controls and keeping the navigation consistent helps these administrative workflows feel like a natural part of the same product.

05 / Reporting

Turn operational activity into information people can review.

Reporting closes the loop by making activity easier to inspect over time. Clear chart hierarchy, labelled metrics and consistent spacing help the data feel approachable instead of overwhelming.

The goal is not decoration: the visualisation should help a user understand what is happening and where to look next.

04 — Design principles

Consistency is the system behind the screens.

Across the platform, the interface needs to feel like one system—even when users move between tables, forms, profile views, configuration and reporting.

01 / HIERARCHYMake the next action obvious.

Use clear headings, grouped information and a strong primary action to reduce the effort of scanning each screen.

02 / REUSERepeat familiar patterns.

Consistent navigation, status treatments, spacing and controls help users transfer their understanding between workflows.

03 / FEEDBACKKeep system state legible.

Labels, status indicators and structured records make the current state easier to understand and review.

05 — Reflection

Designing the system around the work.

LaundroMart was a broad product-design effort across dashboard, inventory, transactions, operator views, facility management, camera setup and reporting. The work is best understood as a connected interface system: each workflow has its own purpose, while navigation, hierarchy and interaction patterns remain consistent.
