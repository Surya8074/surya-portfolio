A small physical witness for the little wins in focused work.

Context

Shi+ne checks a Notion task list. When a task is completed, a small mechanical sunflower rises while the screen face celebrates, then the object settles back into a quiet idle state.

The product is designed to acknowledge work already done—not to send reminders, coach the user, or compete with the task at hand.

Opportunity

Focused work has small wins. A desk companion can give those moments a gentle physical signal.

The design space narrowed around a presence that only becomes noticeable when something meaningful happens. It should not need attention to function.

Research

Two kinds of inquiry shaped the direction: a review of existing desk companions and a week of observing task completion in Notion. The product opportunity sat between needy desk pets and passive productivity tools.

Task completion brought a short-lived sense of satisfaction, but the surrounding workspace did little to mark the transition from effort to progress.

Design principles

Witness, not assistant

React to what is already done. Do not remind, suggest, or coach.

Quiet by default

No sound or notifications. The celebration is subtle and local to the desk.

Charm without infantilization

Give the object expression while keeping it at home on an adult’s desk.

Honest hardware

Let the display and mechanical flower be visible parts of the design.

The final concept

The signature gesture is a sunflower lifted by a servo when the task count changes. The screen face blinks, celebrates, and responds to occasional touch interactions.

Interaction model

The screen provides a small set of expressions. The flower and face respond to meaningful changes, then return to an unobtrusive resting state.

Explore the interaction states

Idle

A brief blink occurs on a randomized interval, keeping the screen lively without making it feel restless.

New task

A new item is acknowledged with a small change in the face, without turning into a reminder or notification.

Task complete

A change in the Notion task list triggers a short celebration, then the flower lowers and the device returns to idle.

Single tap

A quick touch gets a tickled expression, which returns to idle after a moment.

Five rapid taps

The face narrows its eyes and ignores further input briefly, giving the object a little character.

Industrial design

The body gives the screen a clear orientation while the deep green lower section reads like a planter. The printed enclosure was iterated to make the LCD bezel and flower mechanism manufacturable.

View industrial-design media

The first prints exposed a sharp internal corner around the display bezel. Rounding the edge and splitting the enclosure into bolted parts helped the geometry print more reliably.

Technical architecture

The ESP32 V2 connects the display, touch input, servo and Wi-Fi task polling. The device uses a 3.5-inch ILI9486 SPI touch LCD, an SG90 micro servo and a USB-C power connection.

Wiring reference

The display and touch controller share SPI data and clock lines but use separate chip-select pins. The LCD backlight uses the 5V rail; the logic uses 3.3V.

Build log (all the hurdles)

Hardware iteration is physical: each mismatch asks for a new measurement, a wire adjustment, or another print.

The LCD refused to wake up

A blank or blue display sent the team back to the panel datasheet and ESP32 pin map to trace the connection errors.

The default driver library targeted the wrong board

The display driver needed configuration for the specific ILI9486 panel and ESP32 pin names.

The 5V backlight problem

The display logic and backlight use different voltage rails. The backlight needs 5V rather than the 3.3V logic supply.

Permanent soldering

Moving from a test setup to soldered connections made every wire placement harder to reverse.

Notion API onboarding

The integration and task database must be configured and shared correctly before the device can read updates.

The animation library failed on compile

Library and board compatibility needed to be resolved before the expression work could be tested on-device.

Black flash between frames

Rendering to an off-screen buffer helped reduce the visible flash during face animation.

Five failed 3D prints

Print orientation, enclosure splits, and a rounded internal corner helped make the casing more reliable.

The flower felt mechanical

The movement timing needed to feel like a small celebration rather than a plain servo motion.

The sound question

The product stayed quiet so that its feedback would not compete with focused work.

Reflections

Hardware makes decisions tangible. A physical connection or fit issue costs time to undo, making iteration slower and more deliberate.

Documentation is part of the artifact. Wiring maps and setup guidance can be as valuable to the next builder as the device itself.

Restraint can be a feature. No sound, no notifications and no extra app helped the object stay present without demanding attention.

Scope creates room to continue. The shipped prototype is one point in a larger space of possible interactions and physical forms.
